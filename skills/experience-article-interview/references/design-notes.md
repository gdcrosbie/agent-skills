# Design notes

Why the skill works the way it does. Read when adapting it, or when a rule seems to get in
the way of a particular article.

## Origin

This is a generic edition of an editorial skill originally written for one author's own
articles. The method was kept; everything that described that author (identity, audiences,
editorial purpose, voice rules, terminology and publishing channels) was moved into the
author profile, which each user supplies. The generic edition carries no one's personal
voice, material or interview transcripts.

## Rules and their reasons

- **Interview before drafting.** A draft written from a title fills gaps with plausible
  generic opinion, which the author then has to argue against. Asking first gets the
  author's actual decisions, constraints and language onto the page.
- **Authorship boundary.** The article presents the author's experience under their name.
  An invented client, figure or opinion is a false statement by them, so gaps are asked
  about, narrowed or acknowledged, never filled.
- **Roughly 5–10 opening questions, in rounds.** Enough to find the substance without
  turning the interview into a form. Reflecting back after each round lets the author
  correct direction early.
- **Challenge before polish.** Testing the main claim against a fair objection improves the
  argument more than copy editing does, and keeps qualifications that change the advice.
- **Structure after discovery.** The real subject often differs from the starting title.
  The default progression is a suggestion, not a template.
- **Separate experience from verifiable fact.** Research can check dates, numbers and
  product claims; only the author can confirm what they did. Both are recorded, separately.
- **Clean handover.** Article copy, research notes and open questions stay apart so nothing
  unresolved is published by accident. A finished draft never implies permission to publish.
- **Social posts stand alone.** A post that only announces an article gives readers little;
  one useful idea in the opening serves both readers who click and those who do not.
  Platform preview lengths change, so any character target is treated as a target, not a rule.
- **Plain-text social files.** Formatting and paragraph breaks are often lost when pasting
  into social platforms; a `.txt` file preserves the agreed wording, but the skill does not
  promise how a platform will render it.

## Defaults versus profile

The default voice is deliberately modest: plain, concrete, no hype. It exists so the skill
works before a profile is complete. Anything more distinctive (spelling variant, punctuation
rules, banned terms, how the author is described) belongs in the profile, because it
differs between authors and should not be imposed on everyone.

## Deliberately outside scope

No website schema, CMS integration, deployment step, publishing schedule or content
calendar. No particular product, agent or paid service is required. The templates are
optional working aids, not mandatory stages or article formats. The skill does not publish,
upload or post anything.
